Reading (45 minutes) and Writing (45 minutes)

Writing Part 1 — Email

Email submissions go to oxfampreparation@email.cz through FormSubmit. The first submission requires one-time address verification; FormSubmit retains submissions for up to 30 days.

Write about 100 words. Answer your friend and include all three notes.

Your English friend Riley has emailed you.

“Our school is planning an evening event for families. What activity should we include, what could students prepare, and how can we make sure everyone feels welcome?”

Write an email to Riley. In your email, you should:
• suggest an activity
• say what students could prepare
• explain how to welcome different visitors.

Writing Part 2 — Choose one question

Write about 100 words. Answer either Question 2 (article) or Question 3 (story), not both.

Question 2 — Article
Your school magazine wants an article about a useful invention. Describe an invention you use and explain how it makes life easier.

Question 3 — Story
Your story must begin with this sentence:
Just as the final bell rang, the envelope slid under the classroom door.

Speaking (10–12 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners. These are original school-age prompts.

Part 1 — Interview (2–3 minutes)

  • What do you enjoy doing with your family?
  • Which subject at school do you find most interesting?
  • Do you prefer making things or learning how things work?
  • What kind of event would you like your school to organise?
  • Tell me about a time you helped someone learn something.

Part 2 — Extended Turn (3 minutes)

Describe the picture. Say what the young people are making, what they might be discussing, and how they could use it.

Follow-up: Tell your partner about something you have made or would like to make.

Part 3 — Collaborative Task (3–4 minutes)

Your school is planning an open evening for new students and their families. Talk together about these ideas and decide which two activities would help visitors enjoy the evening most.

  • student guided tours
  • short science demonstrations
  • music performances
  • art and design display
  • try-it-yourself classroom activities

Useful questions: Which activities would suit different ages? What can students prepare? Which would be easiest to organise?

Part 4 — Discussion (3 minutes)

  • Why are school events useful for families?
  • Do you think students learn better by making things? Why?
  • What makes a place welcoming to new people?
  • Should schools invite visitors to lessons?
  • What inventions have changed the way people communicate?
