B1 Preliminary for Schools - Test 9 | Original Practice Paper

Reading and Writing Listening Speaking

Reading (45 minutes) and Writing (45 minutes)

Writing Part 1 — Email

Write about 100 words. Answer your friend and include all three notes.

Your English friend Morgan has emailed you.

“Our school is asking students for ideas for a new play space. What feature would you suggest, who should we ask for opinions, and how could we test whether the design works?”

Write an email to Morgan. In your email, you should:
• suggest a feature
• say who to ask
• explain how to test the design.
0 words

Writing Part 2 — Choose one question

Write about 100 words. Answer either Question 2 (article) or Question 3 (story), not both.

Question 2 — Article
Write an article for your school magazine about an outdoor place you enjoy. Describe it and explain what you like doing there.

Question 3 — Story
Your story must begin with this sentence:
When we opened the new gate, the whole class cheered.

0 words

Speaking (10–12 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners. These are original school-age prompts.

Part 1 — Interview (2–3 minutes)

  • What games or activities did you enjoy when you were younger?
  • Do you prefer outdoor places or indoor places?
  • What makes a public place comfortable to use?
  • Have you ever helped improve something at school?
  • What would you add to a park near your home?

Part 2 — Extended Turn (3 minutes)

Describe the picture. Say what the students are doing, why they might be measuring the path, and what they may do next.

Follow-up: Tell your partner about a place where people can spend time together.

Part 3 — Collaborative Task (3–4 minutes)

Your community is planning an outdoor play day for children and families. Talk together about these ideas and decide which two activities would be most enjoyable.

  • simple team games
  • a drawing table
  • a music and movement session
  • a nature trail
  • a picnic area

Useful questions: Which ideas suit different ages? What could families do together? Which activities need the least equipment?

Part 4 — Discussion (3 minutes)

  • Why are parks and playgrounds important for a community?
  • Should children help adults make decisions about play areas?
  • What makes a place easy for everyone to use?
  • Do people need more outdoor spaces in towns?
  • How can neighbours work together to improve their area?
