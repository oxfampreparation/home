Reading and Writing Listening Speaking

Reading (45 minutes) and Writing (45 minutes)

Writing Part 1 — Email

Write about 100 words. Answer your friend and include all three notes.

Your English friend Jamie has emailed you.

“Our class may visit the new transport museum. Which part would you most like to see, what questions could we ask the guide, and how should we travel there?”

Write an email to Jamie. In your email, you should:
• say which exhibit interests you
• suggest questions for the guide
• recommend how to travel.
0 words

Writing Part 2 — Choose one question

Write about 100 words. Answer either Question 2 (article) or Question 3 (story), not both.

Question 2 — Article
Your school website is asking students to write about a place in their town that deserves more visitors. Describe the place and explain why people should go there.

Question 3 — Story
Your story must begin with this sentence:
When the lights went out, I heard a familiar voice from the other side of the hall.

0 words

Speaking (10–12 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners. These are original school-age prompts.

Part 1 — Interview (2–3 minutes)

  • What do you like doing when you have a free afternoon?
  • Do you prefer visiting new places or returning to familiar places? Why?
  • What kind of museum would you enjoy visiting?
  • How do you usually travel around your town?
  • Tell me about something you have learned from an older person.

Part 2 — Extended Turn (3 minutes)

Describe the picture. Say what the young people are doing, what they might be saying, and how they may feel.

Follow-up: Tell your partner about an activity you enjoy doing outside.

Part 3 — Collaborative Task (3–4 minutes)

Your school is organising a charity afternoon. Talk together about these ideas and decide which two activities students would enjoy most.

  • second-hand book table
  • music performance
  • team games
  • student art display
  • snack stall

Useful questions: Which activities are easy to organise? Which could raise money? What would interest different age groups?

Part 4 — Discussion (3 minutes)

  • Why do people enjoy visiting museums?
  • Should students help choose school trips? Why?
  • What can young people learn from doing activities with others?
  • Is it important to know about the history of your town?
  • What makes a good community event?
