Reading and Writing Listening Speaking

Reading (45 minutes) and Writing (45 minutes)

Writing Part 1 — Email

Write about 100 words. Answer your friend and include all three notes.

Your English friend Riley has emailed you.

“I joined the school speaking club, but I’m not sure what to talk about first. What topic should I choose, what could I use to help explain my ideas, and how can I practise?”

Write an email to Riley. In your email, you should:
• suggest a topic
• recommend something to use
• give advice for practising.
0 words

Writing Part 2 — Choose one question

Write about 100 words. Answer either Question 2 (article) or Question 3 (story), not both.

Question 2 — Article
Write an article for your school magazine about a person you admire. Explain who the person is and what you have learned from them.

Question 3 — Story
Your story must begin with this sentence:
The note inside the debate folder was not meant for me.

0 words

Speaking (10–12 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners. These are original school-age prompts.

Part 1 — Interview (2–3 minutes)

  • Do you enjoy sharing your ideas with other people?
  • What topics do you like discussing with friends?
  • Do you prefer working alone or with a partner?
  • What helps you feel confident at school?
  • Tell me about a time you explained something to someone.

Part 2 — Extended Turn (3 minutes)

Describe the picture. Say what the student is doing, what the audience might be thinking, and how the speaker may feel.

Follow-up: Tell your partner about a presentation or talk you remember.

Part 3 — Collaborative Task (3–4 minutes)

Your school is planning a student discussion day. Talk together about these ideas and decide which two would make it interesting for most students.

  • short team debates
  • a guest speaker
  • audience question cards
  • a topic quiz
  • student presentations

Useful questions: Which ideas let more people join in? What needs the least preparation? How could students choose topics?

Part 4 — Discussion (3 minutes)

  • Why is it useful to listen to opinions that are different from your own?
  • Should students discuss school rules with teachers?
  • What makes someone a good speaker?
  • Is it easier to share an opinion in a small group?
  • How can people check whether information is reliable?
