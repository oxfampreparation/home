B1 Preliminary for Schools - Test 3 | Original Practice Paper

Reading and Writing Listening Speaking

Reading (45 minutes) and Writing (45 minutes)

Writing Part 1 — Email

Write about 100 words. Answer the email and include all three notes.

Your English friend Alex has emailed you.

“Our school is organising a special activity day. What activities would you recommend, where could we hold them, and how could we make sure everyone can join in?”

Write an email to Alex. In your email, you should:
• suggest activities for the day
• say where they could take place
• explain how to include students with different interests or abilities.
0 words

Writing Part 2 — Choose one question

Write about 100 words. Answer either Question 2 (article) or Question 3 (story), not both.

Question 2 — Article
Your English teacher has asked you to write an article for the school website.
“A school trip I would recommend”
Write an article saying where students should go, what they could do there, and why the trip would be useful or enjoyable.

Question 3 — Story
Your story must begin with this sentence:
When I opened the box in the art room, I knew our plan would have to change.

0 words

Listening (about 30 minutes)

Speaking (10–12 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners. These are original school-age prompts.

Part 1 — Interview (2–3 minutes)

  • What do you enjoy most about your school?
  • How do you usually prepare for a test?
  • Which school club or activity would you like to try?
  • What do you like doing with your friends at weekends?
  • Tell me about a time you learned something useful outside the classroom.

Part 2 — Extended Turn (3 minutes)

Each candidate talks about one picture for about one minute.

Candidate A: Describe this picture of students preparing a science fair model. What skills might they be learning?

Candidate B: Describe this picture of students organising books in a school library. Why might people enjoy helping in this way?

Part 3 — Collaborative Task (3–4 minutes)

Your school wants to make the library more welcoming at lunchtime. Talk together about these ideas and decide which two would help students most.

Useful questions: Which ideas would cost less? Which would suit different students? Could the school try more than one idea?

Part 4 — Discussion (3 minutes)

  • Do you think students should help make decisions about their school? Why or why not?
  • What makes a good place to study?
  • Is it better to learn something by reading about it or by trying it yourself?
  • How can schools encourage students to read for pleasure?
  • What kind of school project would you like to take part in?
