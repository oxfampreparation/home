Reading and Writing Listening Speaking

Reading (45 minutes) and Writing (45 minutes)

Writing Part 1 — Email

Write about 100 words. Answer your friend and include all three notes.

Your English friend Alex has emailed you.

“I have a lot to do before my exams and sometimes I feel tired. How do you plan your study time, what do you do during breaks, and what helps you relax in the evening?”

Write an email to Alex. In your email, you should:
• explain how you plan your study time
• suggest an activity for a break
• say how you relax.
0 words

Writing Part 2 — Choose one question

Write about 100 words. Answer either Question 2 (article) or Question 3 (story), not both.

Question 2 — Article
Write an article for your school magazine about a small change that made your daily life easier. Explain what you changed and how it helped.

Question 3 — Story
Your story must begin with this sentence:
The alarm rang at exactly the wrong moment, and I knew my plan had to change.

0 words

Listening (about 30 minutes)

Speaking (10–12 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners. These are original school-age prompts.

Part 1 — Interview (2–3 minutes)

  • What do you usually do when you need a break?
  • Do you prefer planning your day or deciding as you go?
  • What helps you feel ready for a busy school day?
  • Which activities help you relax?
  • Tell me about a healthy habit you would like to try.

Part 2 — Extended Turn (3 minutes)

Describe the picture. Say what the students are doing, where they are, and how the activity might help them.

Follow-up: Tell your partner about an activity that helps you feel refreshed.

Part 3 — Collaborative Task (3–4 minutes)

Your school wants to offer more useful ways for students to take a break. Talk together about these ideas and decide which two would help students most.

  • a short walk outside
  • a quiet room for reading
  • gentle stretching
  • a board-game table
  • a calm music corner

Useful questions: Which ideas are easy to organise? What can students do in a short break? Which options suit different people?

Part 4 — Discussion (3 minutes)

  • Why do people need breaks during a busy day?
  • Is it important to have regular routines?
  • What can schools do to help students feel comfortable?
  • Do you think teenagers get enough sleep? Why?
  • How can friends support one another during a difficult week?
