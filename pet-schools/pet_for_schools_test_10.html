B1 Preliminary for Schools - Test 10 | Original Practice Paper

Reading and Writing Listening Speaking

Reading (45 minutes) and Writing (45 minutes)

Writing Part 1 — Email

Write about 100 words. Answer your friend and include all three notes.

Your English friend Mina has emailed you.

“Our class is organising a science fair. What kind of project would you like to show, how could visitors take part, and what should we prepare before the day?”

Write an email to Mina. In your email, you should:
• describe a project
• suggest an activity for visitors
• say what to prepare.
0 words

Writing Part 2 — Choose one question

Write about 100 words. Answer either Question 2 (article) or Question 3 (story), not both.

Question 2 — Article
Write an article for your school magazine about science you notice in everyday life. Give an example and explain why it interests you.

Question 3 — Story
Your story must begin with this sentence:
The small box began to shake just as the visitors arrived.

0 words

Listening (about 30 minutes)

Speaking (10–12 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners. These are original school-age prompts.

Part 1 — Interview (2–3 minutes)

  • What school subject do you find most interesting?
  • Do you enjoy making things with your hands?
  • What do you like doing with friends after school?
  • Have you ever visited a museum or science centre?
  • Would you like to take part in a school fair? Why?

Part 2 — Extended Turn (3 minutes)

Describe the picture. Say what the students are doing, what they might be learning, and what they could show visitors next.

Follow-up: Tell your partner about something you have made or would like to make.

Part 3 — Collaborative Task (3–4 minutes)

Your school is planning an activity area for its science fair. Talk together about these ideas and decide which two would be most interesting for visitors.

  • a simple robot demonstration
  • a paper bridge challenge
  • a weather station display
  • a solar cooking experiment
  • a question corner with a scientist

Useful questions: Which ideas suit younger visitors? Which are easy to prepare? What would visitors learn by trying them?

Part 4 — Discussion (3 minutes)

  • Why is it useful to learn by doing experiments?
  • Should schools invite people from the community to share their knowledge?
  • What can students learn from working in a team?
  • Do you think museums are more interesting for children or adults?
  • How can people use science to solve everyday problems?
