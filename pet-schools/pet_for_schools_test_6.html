Reading and Writing Listening Speaking

Reading (45 minutes) and Writing (45 minutes)

Writing Part 1 — Email

Write about 100 words. Answer your friend and include all three notes.

Your English friend Casey has emailed you.

“Our class is visiting the coastal rescue centre. Which animal would you most like to learn about, what could we ask the guide, and what should we do to prepare for the visit?”

Write an email to Casey. In your email, you should:
• choose an animal
• suggest questions for the guide
• say how to prepare.
0 words

Writing Part 2 — Choose one question

Write about 100 words. Answer either Question 2 (article) or Question 3 (story), not both.

Question 2 — Article
Write an article for your school website about one simple way people can protect the environment. Explain what people can do and why it matters.

Question 3 — Story
Your story must begin with this sentence:
The boat was almost back at the harbour when the radio crackled with a message.

0 words

Listening (about 30 minutes)

Speaking (10–12 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners. These are original school-age prompts.

Part 1 — Interview (2–3 minutes)

  • What animals do you find interesting?
  • Have you ever visited an aquarium or wildlife centre?
  • Do you prefer learning from a book or seeing something in person?
  • What can people do to reduce rubbish?
  • Tell me about a place outdoors that you enjoy visiting.

Part 2 — Extended Turn (3 minutes)

Describe the picture. Say what the young people are doing, what they may be learning, and how they might feel.

Follow-up: Tell your partner about an animal you would like to learn more about.

Part 3 — Collaborative Task (3–4 minutes)

An aquarium is planning a family open day. Talk together about these ideas and decide which two would help visitors learn most.

  • a short keeper talk
  • a sea-life quiz
  • a model-making table
  • a feeding demonstration
  • a guided beach walk

Useful questions: Which ideas suit different ages? What could visitors try themselves? Which activities are easiest to organise?

Part 4 — Discussion (3 minutes)

  • Why do people enjoy seeing animals in person?
  • How can schools teach children to care for nature?
  • Should shops use less plastic packaging? Why?
  • What can families do together outdoors?
  • Why is it useful to learn about local wildlife?
