B1 Preliminary for Schools - Test 5 | Original Practice Paper

Reading and Writing Listening Speaking

Reading (45 minutes) and Writing (45 minutes)

Writing Part 1 — Email

Write about 100 words. Answer your friend and include all three notes.

Your English friend Alex has emailed you.

“I want to join a climbing class, but I’m not sure where to start. What should I bring, which day would be best, and who could come with me?”

Write an email to Alex. In your email, you should:
• recommend what to bring
• suggest a suitable day
• say who could join Alex.
0 words

Writing Part 2 — Choose one question

Write about 100 words. Answer either Question 2 (article) or Question 3 (story), not both.

Question 2 — Article
Write an article for your school magazine about a challenge you tried. Explain what you did and what you learned from it.

Question 3 — Story
Your story must begin with this sentence:
When I reached for the next handhold, I noticed a small message behind it.

0 words

Speaking (10–12 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners. These are original school-age prompts.

Part 1 — Interview (2–3 minutes)

  • What do you enjoy doing to stay active?
  • Do you like trying activities that are difficult at first?
  • Would you rather exercise indoors or outdoors? Why?
  • How do you usually encourage a friend?
  • Tell me about a time you learned a new skill.

Part 2 — Extended Turn (3 minutes)

Describe the picture. Say what the young people are doing, what they might be saying, and how they may feel.

Follow-up: Tell your partner about a challenge you would like to try.

Part 3 — Collaborative Task (3–4 minutes)

A youth centre wants to offer a new weekend programme. Talk together about these ideas and decide which two would be most popular.

  • indoor climbing session
  • beginner dance class
  • team puzzle challenge
  • outdoor nature walk
  • craft workshop

Useful questions: Which activities need special equipment? What would suit beginners? Which options could friends do together?

Part 4 — Discussion (3 minutes)

  • Why do some people enjoy challenging sports?
  • Should schools offer more activities after lessons?
  • Is it easier to learn a skill alone or with a group?
  • How can friends help each other feel confident?
  • What makes a good instructor?
