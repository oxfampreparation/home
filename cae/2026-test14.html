CAE 2026 - Test 14

Reading and Use of EnglishListeningWritingSpeaking

Reading and Use of English (90 minutes)

Listening (about 40 minutes)

The recordings are read aloud by your browser. Each recording can be played twice, as in the real exam. Use headphones.

Writing (90 minutes)

Part 1 - Essay (compulsory)

Your class has attended a lecture on life in modern cities. You have made notes on three points: the cost of housing, air quality and noise, access to services and culture. Write an essay for your tutor discussing TWO of the points. Say which presents the greater challenge and why. Write 220-260 words.

0 words

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

(a) Report: a university committee is reviewing student accommodation; write a report assessing the current situation and recommending improvements. (b) Article: a travel magazine has asked readers to describe a journey they would recommend to others; write an article explaining what makes the journey worthwhile. (c) Proposal: your employer is considering a mentoring scheme for new staff; write a proposal explaining how it could work and why it would be beneficial. Write 220-260 words.

0 words

Sending opens your email app with your text. Very long texts are copied to the clipboard so you can paste them.

Speaking (15 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test lasts about 15 minutes.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • What do you enjoy most about the place where you grew up?
  • Tell us about something you have learned recently.
  • How important is it for you to have a daily routine?
  • Describe an occasion when you had to be patient.

Part 2 - Long turn (1 minute each)

Compare the two pictures and say what the advantages and disadvantages of celebrating in these ways might be. Then answer: which would you prefer for an important birthday, and why?

Part 3 - Collaborative task (3 minutes + 1 minute decision)

How can a local community make its town centre more attractive? Discuss the ideas below, then decide which TWO would make the biggest difference.

  • markets and street events
  • more green spaces
  • restoring historic buildings
  • cheaper parking
  • cultural venues such as theatres and galleries

Part 4 - Discussion (4 minutes)

  • Do traditions matter as much to young people as they did in the past?
  • Is it more important for a town to preserve its history or to focus on the future?
  • How might changing shopping habits affect the appearance of towns?

The pictures are simple drawings. Replace them with photographs you have the right to use.
