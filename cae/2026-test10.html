CAE 2026 - Test 10

Reading and Use of EnglishListeningWritingSpeaking

Reading and Use of English (90 minutes)

Listening (about 40 minutes)

The recordings are read aloud by your browser. Each recording can be played twice, as in the real exam. Use headphones.

Writing (90 minutes)

Part 1 - Essay (compulsory)

Your class has attended a lecture on the role of sport in society. You have made notes on three points: the high salaries of professional athletes, sport in schools, sport as a way of bringing communities together. Write an essay for your tutor discussing TWO of the points. Say which is more important and why. Write 220-260 words.

0 words

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

(a) Proposal: your council owns an empty building in the town centre and wants ideas for using it; write a proposal for the council. (b) Review: write a review of a museum, exhibition or historic site you have visited, saying whether you would recommend it. (c) Letter: you have seen an advertisement for volunteers at a local arts festival; write a letter of application explaining why you would be suitable. Write 220-260 words.

0 words

Sending opens your email app with your text. Very long texts are copied to the clipboard so you can paste them.

Speaking (15 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test lasts about 15 minutes.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • Tell us about the town or city where you live.
  • What do you usually do to relax after a busy day?
  • Describe a journey you have enjoyed.
  • Is there anything you would like to change about the way you spend your time?

Part 2 - Long turn (1 minute each)

Compare the two pictures and say what the pleasures and drawbacks of spending free time in these ways might be. Then answer: which would you find more rewarding, and why?

Part 3 - Collaborative task (3 minutes + 1 minute decision)

How can a town make life better for its older residents? Discuss the ideas below, then decide which TWO would make the biggest difference.

  • accessible public transport
  • community centres
  • safer streets and pavements
  • volunteering schemes
  • affordable leisure activities

Part 4 - Discussion (4 minutes)

  • Do older people have more to offer society than we recognise?
  • Is it the family's or the state's responsibility to care for the elderly?
  • How might cities change as their populations grow older?

The pictures are simple drawings. Replace them with photographs you have the right to use.
