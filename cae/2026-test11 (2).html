CAE 2026 - Test 11

Reading and Use of EnglishListeningWritingSpeaking

Reading and Use of English (90 minutes)

Listening (about 40 minutes)

The recordings are read aloud by your browser. Each recording can be played twice, as in the real exam. Use headphones.

Writing (90 minutes)

Part 1 - Essay (compulsory)

Your class has attended a lecture on how technology is changing healthcare. You have made notes on three ideas: online consultations, wearable fitness devices, artificial intelligence in diagnosis. Write an essay for your tutor discussing TWO of the ideas. Say which has greater potential and why. Write 220-260 words.

0 words

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

(a) Report: your town council wants to attract more young people to the local theatre; write a report with recommendations. (b) Article: a magazine wants readers' articles about a book that changed the way they think; write your article. (c) Letter: write a letter of application for a job as a summer camp leader. Write 220-260 words.

0 words

Sending opens your email app with your text. Very long texts are copied to the clipboard so you can paste them.

Speaking (15 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test lasts about 15 minutes.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • How do you usually spend your lunch break?
  • What is the most interesting place you have visited?
  • What do you like about learning English?
  • How do you stay in touch with your friends?

Part 2 - Long turn (1 minute each)

Compare the two pictures and say why people might choose to exercise in these ways. Then answer: which kind of exercise do you think is better for you, and why?

Part 3 - Collaborative task (3 minutes + 1 minute decision)

What could be done to encourage young people to vote? Discuss the ideas below, then decide which TWO would be most effective.

  • voting online
  • lowering the voting age
  • lessons about politics in schools
  • campaigns by famous people
  • making voting compulsory

Part 4 - Discussion (4 minutes)

  • Does democracy need citizens who are well informed?
  • How much influence does social media have on politics?
  • Should voting be compulsory?

The pictures are simple drawings. Replace them with photographs you have the right to use.
