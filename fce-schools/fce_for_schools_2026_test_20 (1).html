Reading and Use of English Listening Writing Speaking

Reading and Use of English (75 minutes)

Listening (about 40 minutes)

The recordings are read aloud by your browser. Each recording can be played twice, as in the real exam. Use headphones.

Writing (80 minutes)

Part 1 - Essay (compulsory)

In your English class you have been talking about school sports and student health. Now, your English teacher has asked you to write an essay for homework.

Write an essay using all the notes and giving reasons for your point of view.

Topic: Should participation in team sports be compulsory for all students at secondary school?

Notes:
Write about:
1. physical fitness and health
2. teamwork and social skills
3. ___________ (your own idea)

Write your essay in 140 - 190 words.

0 words

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 140 - 190 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Article: You see this announcement in an English-language magazine for young people: Articles wanted: Learning a Musical Instrument! Why is it worthwhile for teenagers to learn to play an instrument? What are the biggest challenges, and how can young people overcome them? The best articles will be published next month.

(b) Story: Your English teacher has asked you to write a story for the school website. Your story must begin with this sentence: As soon as Leo stepped onto the stage, he knew this performance would be completely different from all the others.

(c) Review: A new escape room or puzzle attraction has recently opened in your town and is very popular with teenagers. Write a review for an English-language teen blog. Describe the challenges and the atmosphere, explain what you liked and disliked, and say whether you would recommend it to your friends.

0 words

Speaking (14 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test lasts about 14 minutes.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • Tell us about your favourite place in your hometown. Why do you like it there?
  • How do you usually spend your weekday evenings after finishing school?
  • What are your plans for your further education or career when you finish school?
  • Do you enjoy watching films at home or going to the cinema? Why?

Part 2 - Long turn (1 minute each)

Candidate A: Compare the two pictures and say what you think the teenagers are enjoying about working outdoors in these different situations.

Candidate B: Compare the two pictures and say why you think the teenagers have chosen to spend their free time in these different places.

Candidate A's pictures: Working outdoors

Candidate B's pictures: Spending free time

Part 3 - Collaborative task (3 minutes)

How can a school help its students feel less stressed about their exams? Discuss the ideas below, then decide which TWO would be the most effective.

  • providing workshops on effective study methods
  • organising fun relaxation activities during study breaks
  • reducing the amount of homework given before exams
  • offering one-on-one revision support sessions with teachers
  • inviting mental health professionals to give wellness talks

Part 4 - Discussion (4 minutes)

  • Some people say that exams are the only fair way to test a student's knowledge. Do you agree? Why/Why not?
  • How important is it for teenagers to maintain a good balance between studying and socialising?
  • Do you think schools should teach teenagers mental health and stress-management techniques? Why/Why not?
