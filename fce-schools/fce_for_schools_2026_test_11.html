FCE for Schools 2026 - Test 11

Reading and Use of English Listening Writing Speaking

Reading and Use of English (75 minutes)

Listening (about 40 minutes)

The recordings are read aloud by your browser. Each recording can be played twice, as in the real exam. Use headphones.

Writing (80 minutes)

Part 1 - Essay (compulsory)

In your English class you have been talking about teenage employment. Now, your English teacher has asked you to write an essay for homework.

Write an essay using all the notes and giving reasons for your point of view.

Topic: Should teenagers have a part-time job while they are still studying at school?

Notes:
Write about:
1. learning about responsibility and money
2. having enough time for homework
3. ___________ (your own idea)

Write your essay in 140 - 190 words.

0 words

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 140 - 190 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Article: You see this announcement in an English-language magazine for young people: Articles wanted: A Memorable School Event! Tell us about an event at your school that you will never forget, like a sports day, a play, or a special assembly. What happened, and why was it so special? The best articles will be published next month.

(b) Story: Your English teacher has asked you to write a story for the school website. Your story must begin with this sentence: As the lights went out in the museum, Ben realised he was completely alone.

(c) Review: You recently watched a film or television series about a historical event. Write a review for your school magazine. Describe the story, explain what you liked or disliked about the way history was portrayed, and say whether you would recommend it to other students.

0 words

Sending opens your email app with your text. Very long texts are copied to the clipboard so you can paste them.

Speaking (14 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test lasts about 14 minutes.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • Tell us about the area where you live. What do you like most about it?
  • How often do you use social media, and what do you use it for?
  • Tell us about a hobby you have recently started or would like to try.
  • Do you prefer to spend your holidays in a city or in the countryside?

Part 2 - Long turn (1 minute each)

Candidate A: Compare the two pictures and say what you think the teenagers are enjoying about studying in these different ways.

Candidate B: Compare the two pictures and say why you think the teenagers have chosen to relax in these different environments.

Candidate A's pictures: Studying

Candidate B's pictures: Relaxing

Part 3 - Collaborative task (3 minutes)

How could your school provide better after-school activities for teenagers? Discuss the ideas below, then decide which TWO would be the most popular with students.

  • setting up a modern e-sports and gaming club
  • organising regular cooking and baking classes
  • starting a drama and theatre group
  • providing an indoor climbing wall
  • running a digital photography and editing course

Part 4 - Discussion (4 minutes)

  • Do you think schools should make after-school clubs compulsory? Why/Why not?
  • Some people say that teenagers spend too much time on academic work and not enough time developing practical hobbies. Do you agree?
  • How important is it for young people to socialise with students from different classes or age groups?
