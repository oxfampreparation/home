CPE 2026 - Test 10 (C2 Proficiency)

Reading and Use of English Listening Writing Speaking

Reading and Use of English (1 hour 30 minutes)

Listening (about 40 minutes)

The recordings are read aloud by your browser. Each recording can be played twice, as in the real exam. Use headphones.

Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: The Promise of Precision Agriculture
To sustain a burgeoning global population, agriculture must undergo a rapid, technology-driven paradigm shift. Traditional farming is chronically inefficient, wasting immense volumes of water and saturating soils with broad-spectrum fertilizers. Precision agriculture, powered by AI, satellite imagery, and autonomous drones, offers a definitive solution. By meticulously analyzing micro-climates and applying nutrients only where statistically required, we can dramatically exponentially increase crop yields while virtually eliminating environmental runoff. Clinging to romanticized, low-yield traditional methods is an indulgence we can no longer afford in the face of widespread ecological degradation and impending global food shortages.

Text 2: The Perils of Agritech Dependency
The uncritical embrace of highly capitalized agricultural technology threatens to irrevocably fracture rural communities and centralize food production into the hands of a few corporate monopolies. While algorithmic farming boasts theoretical efficiency, it fundamentally severs the farmer’s intimate, intuitive connection to the land. Furthermore, the immense capital required to procure and maintain proprietary 'smart' machinery forces independent farmers into crippling debt cycles, accelerating the death of the family farm. True sustainability relies on regenerative, locally adapted practices passed down through generations, not on fragile technological infrastructures that fail catastrophically during localized power outages or software glitches.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Proposal: Your local municipal council is offering a substantial grant for a project aimed at reducing urban social isolation among the elderly. Write a proposal outlining a comprehensive community initiative. You should detail the logistical setup of a physical space, the integration of intergenerational mentoring, and provide a robust justification for why your specific approach will yield long-term psychological benefits for the participants.

(b) Review: A technology magazine is commissioning reviews on the theme 'Hardware that Changed the World'. Choose a specific, historical piece of consumer technology (e.g., the first mass-produced digital camera, an early portable cassette player). Write a review evaluating its initial cultural impact, analyzing how its design influenced human behaviour, and concluding on its enduring legacy in modern society.

(c) Letter: A renowned educational journal recently published an article stating that 'The teaching of cursive handwriting is an archaic, useless pursuit in an era dominated by keyboard fluency, and should be entirely abolished from primary education.' Write a letter to the editor fiercely opposing this view, arguing for the cognitive, fine-motor, and historical benefits of maintaining handwriting instruction in the digital age.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways do you feel global travel has lost its sense of genuine discovery?
  • How important is the physical design of a workspace to your personal productivity?
  • To what extent do you believe modern society has become overly risk-averse?
  • If you could witness the construction of any historical monument, which would it be and why?

Part 2 - Collaborative Task (4 minutes)

Here are some photographs illustrating vastly different approaches to urban architecture. First, talk together about what these structural choices suggest about the values of the societies that built them. Then, decide which architectural style fosters a stronger sense of communal identity.

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

Candidate A (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate B will then answer a question.

To what extent is the pursuit of absolute space colonization a dangerous distraction?
  • The diversion of vital funding from Earth's climate crises
  • The inevitability of carrying human conflicts to new planets
  • The fundamental evolutionary drive to explore and expand

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe the commercialization of space travel undermines its scientific nobility?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

How is the concept of 'work-life balance' fundamentally shifting in the modern economy?
  • The dissolution of boundaries caused by remote working
  • The rise of the precarious 'gig economy'
  • The push towards a mandated four-day working week

Question for Candidate A: Is the idea of a 'career for life' now entirely a historical anomaly?

General Discussion:

  • Some economists argue that universal basic income is an inevitable necessity due to automation. What is your assessment of this?
  • How might the preservation of endangered languages actively benefit scientific research?
  • Does the constant documentation of our lives on social media degrade the actual quality of our memories?
