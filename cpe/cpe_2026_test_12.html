Reading and Use of English Listening Writing Speaking

Reading and Use of English (1 hour 30 minutes)

Listening (about 40 minutes)

The recordings are read aloud by your browser. Each recording can be played twice, as in the real exam. Use headphones.

Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: The Imperative of Space Exploration
In an era increasingly defined by resource scarcity and ecological fragility on Earth, the aggressive funding of space exploration is not merely a scientific luxury, but an absolute existential imperative. Establishing robust, sustainable off-world colonies and developing the capacity for asteroid mining provides humanity with a vital insurance policy against planetary catastrophes, be they anthropogenic or cosmic in nature. Furthermore, the technological innovations demanded by the harsh environment of space—from advanced water purification to high-yield hydroponics—invariably trickle down to solve pressing humanitarian crises on Earth, proving that looking upwards is often the most pragmatic way to move forwards.

Text 2: The Abyss Over the Heavens
The obsession with colonizing desolate, irradiated planets is a profound misallocation of human ingenuity and capital, especially when our own oceans remain vastly unexplored. The deep sea, representing over ninety percent of the Earth's habitable space, holds the immediate answers to our most critical challenges. The uncharted biodiversity of the abyssal plains contains the key to revolutionary pharmaceuticals and sustainable biotechnologies. Investing in marine archaeology and deep-sea preservation yields tangible, immediate benefits for global ecosystems, whereas chasing utopian martian colonies merely allows billionaires to escape the very environmental devastation they have historically profited from. We must look inward to our own depths before looking outward to the stars.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Proposal: Your city council is accepting proposals for the radical regeneration of a vast, abandoned industrial park located near the city centre. Write a proposal outlining a comprehensive, multi-use project that prioritizes ecological sustainability. You should detail specific facilities that will serve the local community, evaluate how the project will be financially self-sustaining after the initial grant, and justify why your design will foster a strong sense of civic pride.

(b) Review: A prominent cultural magazine is running a special feature on 'Theatre in the Digital Age'. They are seeking reviews of live stage productions that successfully incorporate advanced technology (such as holograms, live video feeds, or interactive sets) without overshadowing the human performance. Write a review describing a production you have seen, evaluating the effectiveness of its technological elements, and analyzing how they enhanced the emotional resonance of the narrative.

(c) Letter: A national newspaper has published a controversial editorial arguing that 'In an era of universal digital access to information, physical libraries are an obsolete drain on municipal taxes and should be entirely permanently closed'. Write a letter to the editor fiercely opposing this view, arguing for the irreplaceable sociological and educational roles that physical libraries play, particularly for marginalized communities, and defending them as essential pillars of democratic society.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways do you feel your generation is more, or less, politically engaged than previous ones?
  • How important is it for individuals to continually re-evaluate their core beliefs?
  • To what extent do you believe the traditional university model needs to be reformed?
  • If you were given unlimited resources to solve one localized community issue, what would you choose?

Part 2 - Collaborative Task (4 minutes)

Here are two photographs illustrating environments where massive amounts of complex information are processed. First, talk together about what specific pressures these completely different environments place on the people working within or managing them. Then, decide which environment represents a greater vulnerability to catastrophic systemic failure.

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

Candidate A (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate B will then answer a question.

To what extent should healthcare be treated as a fundamental human right rather than a commodity?
  • The moral obligation of the state towards vulnerable citizens
  • The financial sustainability of entirely free, universal systems
  • The impact of privatization on the quality of medical innovation

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe the pharmaceutical industry holds too much influence over public health policy?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

How is the phenomenon of mass global tourism impacting historically significant sites?
  • The economic revitalization of culturally neglected regions
  • The physical degradation and commodification of ancient monuments
  • The ethical dilemma of restricting access based on wealth or quotas

Question for Candidate A: Is 'eco-tourism' largely a superficial marketing term, or does it offer genuine conservation benefits?

General Discussion:

  • Some cultural critics argue that the modern obsession with 'mindfulness' is just a way to tolerate an inherently toxic work culture. What is your assessment of this?
  • How might the widespread adoption of lab-grown synthetic food alter humanity's relationship with the natural world?
  • Does the constant pursuit of economic growth inevitably conflict with the goal of genuine environmental sustainability?
