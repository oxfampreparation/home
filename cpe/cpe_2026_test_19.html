Reading and Use of English Listening Writing Speaking

Reading and Use of English (1 hour 30 minutes)

Listening (about 40 minutes)

The recordings are read aloud by your browser. Each recording can be played twice, as in the real exam. Use headphones.

Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: The Promise of Global Integration
The accelerating forces of economic globalization have undeniably forged a deeply interconnected, remarkably resilient international architecture. By transcending rigid national borders, the free flow of capital, goods, and human expertise has exponentially accelerated technological innovation and lifted hundreds of millions out of absolute poverty in developing nations. Furthermore, this intense geopolitical interdependence acts as a vital deterrent to large-scale military conflict, as nations are economically disincentivized from attacking their primary trading partners. Embracing a unified, globalized worldview is an unavoidable necessity if humanity hopes to effectively tackle borderless crises, such as climate change or international pandemics, through coordinated, multilateral action.

Text 2: The Return to Localism
While the theoretical benefits of a borderless economy are frequently lauded by the global elite, the brutal reality of unchecked globalization is the severe devastation of domestic manufacturing and working-class stability. By ruthlessly outsourcing industrial production to nations with dismal labor regulations, multi-national conglomerates maximize shareholder profit while decimating local communities. In response, a powerful movement advocating for 'radical localism' is emerging. This philosophy aggressively prioritizes regional self-sufficiency, resilient, short-distance supply chains, and the passionate defense of unique, indigenous cultural identities against the bland, homogenous erosion driven by global corporate monopolies. True sustainability requires a return to localized, deeply rooted civic economies.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Proposal: Your city is planning to launch a highly ambitious 'community solar grid' project to decentralize energy production. You have been asked to submit a proposal outlining the logistical implementation. Detail how residents will financially participate, analyze the potential architectural challenges of retrofitting historic neighborhoods, and justify why this project will foster a greater sense of ecological responsibility.

(b) Review: A prominent cultural website is curating a list of 'Science Fiction Films that Predicted Modern Bioethics'. Write a review of a film you have seen that deals heavily with themes of artificial intelligence or genetic engineering. Evaluate how effectively the director portrays the moral ambiguity of the scientists involved, and analyze whether the film’s central warning feels genuinely prophetic or merely alarmist.

(c) Letter: Your regional government is quietly considering a proposal to privatize a massive, pristine natural lake to a multinational corporation for commercial water bottling. Write a letter to the editor of your national newspaper fiercely opposing this action. You should defend the concept of public ownership of vital natural resources, outline the devastating long-term ecological consequences of industrial extraction, and urge immediate legislative intervention.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways do you believe our perception of 'privacy' has fundamentally shifted in the digital age?
  • How important is it for modern cities to retain elements of their ancient architectural history?
  • To what extent do you feel that the traditional educational system suppresses innate human creativity?
  • If you could permanently eliminate one aspect of modern consumer culture, what would it be?

Part 2 - Collaborative Task (4 minutes)

Here are two photographs illustrating profoundly different ways people engage with vast amounts of information. First, talk together about the specific physical and cognitive experiences of absorbing knowledge in these contrasting environments. Then, decide which environment offers a superior method for cultivating deep, sustained analytical thought.

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

Candidate A (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate B will then answer a question.

To what extent does the modern pursuit of extreme longevity threaten societal progress?
  • The resulting economic stagnation caused by an aging, entrenched workforce
  • The profound ethical dilemma of limiting life-extending technologies to the wealthy
  • The psychological impact of living in a world without natural generational turnover

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe the state has a moral obligation to fund anti-aging research?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

How is the increasing reliance on algorithmic surveillance altering human behaviour in public spaces?
  • The chilling effect on legitimate political protest and civil disobedience
  • The willingness of citizens to trade absolute privacy for perceived security
  • The inherent, dangerous biases frequently programmed into facial recognition software

Question for Candidate A: Is the expectation of anonymity in a modern urban environment completely obsolete?

General Discussion:

  • Some sociologists argue that 'hustle culture' is a toxic, modern mechanism designed to exploit the working class. What is your assessment?
  • How might the widespread discovery of extraterrestrial microbial life fundamentally alter major world religions?
  • Does the intense modern focus on 'wellness' actually create a toxic culture of narcissistic self-obsession?
