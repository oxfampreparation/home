Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: The Case for Public Transport Investment
The inexorable expansion of global urban centres demands a radical pivot away from the private automobile. Governments must aggressively channel infrastructure funds into comprehensive, highly subsidized public transit systems. Beyond the obvious mitigation of disastrous ecological impacts and suffocating urban pollution, robust public transport fosters profound social cohesion. It dismantles the socioeconomic segregation inherently enforced by car dependency, creating shared civic spaces where individuals from disparate backgrounds interact daily. Prioritizing trains and buses over highways is not merely an environmental imperative; it is a fundamental requisite for a genuinely egalitarian, smoothly functioning metropolis.

Text 2: The Autonomous Revolution
The nostalgic clamour for expanding massive, inflexible public railway networks fails to acknowledge the impending revolution of autonomous vehicles (AVs). The future of urban mobility does not lie in forcing commuters onto rigid, overcrowded timetables, but in the deployment of highly efficient, algorithmic fleets of self-driving electric pods. These systems offer point-to-point convenience, radically minimizing the 'last-mile' friction that plagues traditional transit. Furthermore, AV networks, communicating instantaneously to optimize traffic flow, will virtually eradicate congestion and human-error fatalities. Funneling billions into outdated train infrastructure when we are on the cusp of an individualized transport utopia is a colossal misallocation of resources.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Letter: A major national newspaper recently published an editorial arguing that 'Funding for the arts is an unjustifiable luxury during times of economic austerity, and state subsidies should be entirely diverted to essential healthcare and infrastructure.' You decide to write a letter to the editor fiercely challenging this view, evaluating the intrinsic societal value of art and arguing for its necessity even during financial crises.

(b) Review: A prominent literary magazine is running a special feature on 'Historical Biographies that Resonate Today'. They are seeking reviews of biographies that offer profound insights into modern political or social issues. Write a review describing the biography you read, analyzing how the author brings the historical figure to life, and evaluating why their story remains deeply relevant to contemporary audiences.

(c) Article: An online sociology journal is seeking articles on 'The Psychological Toll of Constant Connectivity'. They want pieces examining the creeping anxiety and burnout associated with modern digital life. Write an article outlining the primary cognitive drivers of this exhaustion, evaluating the subtle ways hyper-connectivity diminishes deep, focused thought, and proposing radical strategies for individuals to reclaim their mental autonomy.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways have your everyday habits changed over the last five years?
  • How significant is the role of historical knowledge in understanding current global events?
  • To what extent do you believe that face-to-face interaction will be permanently diminished by virtual reality?
  • If you could guarantee the success of one major personal ambition, what would it be?

Part 2 - Collaborative Task (4 minutes)

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

To what extent should governments regulate content on the internet?
  • The suppression of dangerous misinformation
  • The preservation of absolute free speech
  • The practical challenges of enforcing global censorship

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe that anonymity on the internet is a fundamental human right?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

How might the eradication of physical cash alter the fabric of society?
  • The potential for total state surveillance of transactions
  • The marginalization of digitally illiterate populations
  • The reduction of petty crime and the black market

Question for Candidate A: Can a truly cashless society ever guarantee robust economic privacy?

General Discussion:

  • Some cultural theorists argue that genuine originality in art and literature is no longer possible. What is your assessment of this?
  • How might the increasing reliance on algorithmic decision-making in the justice system compromise human morality?
  • Is the concept of the traditional 'nation-state' becoming obsolete in an era of borderless digital economies?
