Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: A Necessity for Automation
As artificial intelligence and robotics accelerate, the traditional link between human labour and economic survival is fracturing. A Universal Basic Income (UBI)—an unconditional, regular cash payment given to all citizens regardless of employment status—is no longer a utopian fantasy but a structural necessity. By providing a financial safety net, UBI eradicates the absolute poverty caused by technological displacement. Furthermore, it liberates individuals from the coercion of menial, exploitative labour, allowing them to pursue education, creative endeavours, or vital unpaid care work, thereby fostering a more humane and intellectually vibrant society.

Text 2: The Economic Fallacy
Proponents of Universal Basic Income often ignore the staggering macroeconomic realities of implementing such a system. Distributing a meaningful living wage to an entire population would require punitive levels of taxation, effectively stifling entrepreneurial innovation and driving capital flight. Moreover, the psychological consequences of severing income from productive contribution are profoundly concerning. Work provides human beings not only with sustenance, but with a fundamental sense of purpose, identity, and social integration. A society subsidized by unconditional stipends risks plunging into widespread apathy and civic stagnation.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Letter: An international arts magazine recently published an editorial arguing that 'Artificial Intelligence algorithms capable of generating flawless paintings and symphonies will inevitably render human artistic expression obsolete and culturally irrelevant within a decade'. You decide to write a letter to the editor responding to this claim, evaluating the unique value of human creativity versus machine generation.

(b) Review: A literary website is running a special feature on 'Reassessing the Classics'. They are inviting readers to submit a review of a widely celebrated classic novel that they feel is highly overrated. Write a review describing the book, analyzing why its plot or characters fail to resonate with modern audiences, and evaluating whether its classic status is still justified.

(c) Article: An academic journal is compiling an edition entitled 'The Commercialisation of the Cosmos'. They are seeking articles examining the recent surge in private space exploration. Write an article outlining the potential scientific benefits of corporate space travel, evaluating the ethical risks of allowing billionaires to dictate orbital policy, and proposing a framework for responsible cosmic expansion.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways do you think your cultural background has shaped your worldview?
  • How crucial is it for society to invest in the exploration of the deep ocean?
  • To what extent do you believe that modern convenience has eroded human resilience?
  • If you could witness the resolution of one major scientific mystery in your lifetime, what would it be?

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

  • The psychological impact of omnipresent tracking

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe that individuals have willingly surrendered their right to anonymity for the sake of convenience?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

How vital is a profound understanding of history for navigating contemporary geopolitical crises?
  • The cyclical nature of human conflict
  • The danger of historical revisionism
  • Applying past resolutions to modern diplomacy

Question for Candidate A: Can technological progress eventually render historical precedents irrelevant?

General Discussion:

  • Some sociologists argue that the concept of 'truth' has been irrevocably fragmented by social media algorithms. What is your assessment?
  • How might the widespread genetic modification of human beings alter our philosophical definition of equality?
  • Is the pursuit of continuous, exponential economic growth fundamentally incompatible with long-term ecological survival?
