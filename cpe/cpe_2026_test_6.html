Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: The Promise of Genetic Engineering
The application of genetic engineering in global agriculture represents a profound humanitarian triumph. By deliberately manipulating the genome of staple crops, scientists have engineered varieties that are highly resistant to devastating pests, resilient in the face of escalating droughts, and fortified with essential vitamins. This bio-technological intervention is not a luxury; it is an absolute necessity if we are to feed an exponentially expanding global population while mitigating the catastrophic yield losses triggered by rapid climate change. Rejecting this science out of unfounded fear amounts to condemning millions to malnutrition and agricultural collapse.

Text 2: Tinkering with Nature's Blueprint
The reckless acceleration of genetically modified (GM) crops into the biosphere constitutes a perilous gamble with our ecological security. Proponents conveniently ignore the profound, long-term risks associated with cross-pollination, where engineered traits inevitably contaminate wild plant populations, potentially decimating natural biodiversity. Furthermore, this agricultural paradigm dangerously consolidates power within a handful of multinational agro-chemical monopolies, stripping local farmers of their autonomy and traditional seed-saving practices. True sustainability lies in regenerative, localized farming methods, not in the arrogant assumption that we can flawlessly out-engineer millions of years of natural evolution.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Review: An arts magazine is compiling a feature on 'Challenging Theatre'. They are seeking reviews of contemporary plays that tackle complex or controversial social issues. Write a review describing the play you saw, evaluating the dramatic techniques used to convey its message, and analyzing whether it successfully provoked intellectual debate or merely alienated its audience.

(b) Letter: A national newspaper recently published an editorial arguing that 'Public investment in preserving dilapidated historical buildings is a frivolous waste of taxpayer money that should be entirely redirected towards modern infrastructure and housing.' Write a letter to the editor. In your letter, you should forcefully counter this view, evaluating the irreplaceable psychological and cultural value of architectural heritage, and proposing sustainable economic models for their preservation.

(c) Article: An online cultural journal is seeking articles on 'The Homogenization of Global Culture'. They want pieces examining how the ubiquity of western media and multinational chains is eroding distinct regional identities. Write an article outlining the primary drivers of this cultural flattening, evaluating the psychological impact on local communities, and proposing robust strategies for maintaining authentic cultural diversity in a hyper-connected world.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways do you feel your generation's attitude towards work differs from previous generations?
  • How significant is the role of continuous academic learning in your personal life?
  • To what extent do you believe that social media fosters genuine political engagement?
  • If you could witness the resolution of one major scientific paradox, what would it be?

Part 2 - Collaborative Task (4 minutes)

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

Candidate A (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate B will then answer a question.

How has the globalization of food impacted local cultures?
  • The erosion of traditional culinary practices
  • The homogenization of agricultural landscapes
  • The rise of culinary fusion as cultural dialogue

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe that the concept of 'authentic' national cuisine is a modern myth?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

To what extent should art be subsidized by the state?
  • The intrinsic value of art vs. economic utility
  • The danger of state censorship and propaganda
  • Ensuring accessibility for marginalized communities

Question for Candidate A: Can commercial viability coexist with true artistic integrity?

General Discussion:

  • Some sociologists argue that true privacy is impossible in a society reliant on digital infrastructure. What is your assessment of this?
  • How might the increasing reliance on algorithmic curation in media limit our exposure to challenging or divergent philosophical viewpoints?
  • Is the concept of 'endless economic progress' a fundamentally destructive myth?
