Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: The Four-Day Work Week as Economic Imperative
The traditional five-day, forty-hour work week is a relic of the industrial revolution, fundamentally incompatible with the intense cognitive demands of the modern knowledge economy. Numerous large-scale global trials have categorically demonstrated that transitioning to a four-day week, with no reduction in pay, does not harm productivity. In fact, by mitigating chronic employee burnout and severely reducing absenteeism, corporate output often paradoxically increases. Furthermore, the societal benefits are profound. A mandated shorter working week dramatically reduces commuter-driven carbon emissions and liberates individuals to engage in vital civic duties, caregiving, and community building that a hyper-capitalist schedule systematically stifles.

Text 2: The Illusion of Reduced Labor
While the proposition of a four-day work week is undeniably seductive, it masks a deeply flawed understanding of global economic competitiveness. Attempting to condense forty hours of complex operational output into thirty-two hours inevitably results in a relentless, punishing intensification of the workday. Employees frequently report skipping meals, avoiding collaborative discussions, and experiencing heightened anxiety in a desperate bid to meet unchanged targets within a compressed timeframe. Moreover, this policy disproportionately benefits white-collar professionals while utterly ignoring essential service workers, hospitality staff, and the medical sector, thereby brutally exacerbating existing socio-economic inequalities and creating a dangerous two-tier labor market.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Report: You have been commissioned by the board of a large regional high school to write a report on the feasibility of integrating generative AI tools into the classroom curriculum. You should evaluate the potential improvements in personalized student tutoring, analyze the significant ethical concerns regarding academic plagiarism and critical thinking degradation, and provide a clear recommendation on how to implement a cautious, hybrid pilot program.

(b) Review: A prominent environmental magazine is publishing a special issue on 'Documentaries that Demand Action'. They are seeking reviews of investigative films that tackle the crisis of global microplastic pollution. Write a review of a documentary you have watched, evaluating how effectively the director translates complex ecological data into a compelling, emotive narrative, and analyzing whether the film's ultimate message offers actionable hope or succumbs to fatalism.

(c) Letter: Your local municipal council has voted to sell off a vast, historically significant public park to a private development conglomerate to build luxury, gated apartments, citing the need for increased tax revenue. Write a letter to the editor of your national newspaper fiercely opposing this decision. You should defend the irreplaceable psychological and ecological value of communal green spaces, highlight the democratic necessity of public land, and propose alternative methods for the council to generate civic funds.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways do you believe our perception of 'leisure time' has been distorted by modern technology?
  • How important is it for societies to invest in the exploration of the deep ocean?
  • To what extent do you feel that celebrity culture negatively impacts the political process?
  • If you could permanently eliminate one aspect of the modern education system, what would it be?

Part 2 - Collaborative Task (4 minutes)

Here are two photographs illustrating different forms of human connection in the 21st century. First, talk together about the specific psychological benefits and drawbacks inherent in these contrasting social environments. Then, decide which environment fosters a more profound, enduring sense of personal empathy.

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

Candidate A (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate B will then answer a question.

To what extent does extreme wealth insulate individuals from the reality of the human condition?
  • The creation of hyper-exclusive, privatized living environments
  • The psychological impact of never facing absolute financial precarity
  • The philanthropic obligation of the ultra-wealthy

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe the existence of billionaires is a sign of a failed economic system?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

How is the phenomenon of 'cancel culture' affecting the creation of modern art and literature?
  • The rise of extreme self-censorship among authors and comedians
  • The democratization of holding powerful public figures accountable
  • The psychological toll of enduring an organized digital mob

Question for Candidate A: Should an artist's personal moral failings entirely invalidate their creative work?

General Discussion:

  • Some cultural critics argue that the modern obsession with 'wellness' and self-care is merely a symptom of a deeply sick society. What is your assessment of this?
  • How might the widespread deployment of autonomous military drones permanently alter the ethics of warfare?
  • Does the intense global focus on STEM subjects dangerously devalue the study of human history and philosophy?
