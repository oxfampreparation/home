Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: The Case for a Cashless Society
The inexorable transition towards a fully digitized, cashless economy presents unparalleled advantages for modern governance. Physical currency is inherently inefficient, costly to mint and transport, and serves as the primary lifeblood of the shadow economy. By mandating digital transactions, states can virtually eradicate tax evasion, drastically reduce petty theft, and trace the financial pipelines that fund international syndicates. Furthermore, the friction-less nature of digital payments accelerates global trade, integrating previously unbanked populations into the formal economy via basic mobile technology. Clinging to paper money is an irrational, nostalgic attachment to a hopelessly archaic mechanism of exchange.

Text 2: The Dangers of Digital Currency
The enthusiastic pursuit of a cashless society is a dangerous capitulation to total financial surveillance. Eradicating physical money hands unprecedented, terrifying power over to central banks and private payment processors, allowing them to monitor, analyze, and potentially restrict every single transaction an individual makes. This represents a catastrophic erosion of civic privacy. Moreover, a purely digital economy is fundamentally fragile; a severe cyber-attack or systemic power grid failure would instantly paralyze commerce, leaving millions unable to purchase basic necessities. Physical cash is not merely a tool for commerce; it is a vital, decentralized bulwark against authoritarian overreach and systemic vulnerability.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Report: Your multinational corporation is suffering from a massive drop in employee well-being due to poor workspace design. You have been commissioned by the HR director to write a report evaluating the current office layout. You should analyze the negative psychological impacts of open-plan seating, evaluate the necessity of incorporating 'biophilic' (nature-inspired) design elements, and recommend a structured plan to overhaul the physical environment.

(b) Review: A prominent science magazine is seeking reviews of compelling documentaries about extreme weather events. Write a review of a documentary you have watched, evaluating how successfully the director balances the visceral terror of the event with rigorous meteorological science, and analyzing whether the film ultimately inspires a sense of environmental responsibility or mere fatalism.

(c) Letter: A national newspaper has published an editorial arguing that 'The study of classical philosophy is a self-indulgent waste of university funding and should be entirely replaced by practical coding academies.' Write a letter to the editor fiercely challenging this utilitarian perspective, defending the irreplaceable value of ethical reasoning in the modern era, and arguing that technological advancement is dangerous without a philosophical framework.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways do you feel the concept of a 'traditional family' is changing in your culture?
  • How important is it for individuals to challenge their own deeply held political beliefs?
  • To what extent do you believe that space tourism will eventually become commonplace?
  • If you could mandate a single global law to protect the environment, what would it be?

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

Candidate A (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate B will then answer a question.

To what extent should history be rewritten to reflect modern ethical values?
  • The danger of judging the past by contemporary moral standards
  • The necessity of dismantling glorified narratives of imperialism
  • The educational value of preserving uncomfortable historical truths

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe the removal of controversial statues genuinely helps to heal societal divisions?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

How is the concept of personal privacy evolving in the 21st century?
  • The willingness of the public to trade data for convenience
  • The ubiquitous deployment of facial recognition technology
  • The psychological impact of living under constant digital surveillance

Question for Candidate A: Is the expectation of absolute anonymity on the internet a dangerous illusion?

General Discussion:

  • Some critics argue that modern blockbuster cinema prioritizes visual spectacle over narrative depth. What is your assessment of this?
  • How might the widespread adoption of lab-grown meat fundamentally alter our relationship with animals?
  • Does the constant pressure to be 'productive' in our leisure time actually diminish our overall well-being?
