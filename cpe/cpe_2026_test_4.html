Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: A Catalyst for Innovation
The expenditure allocated to space exploration is frequently lambasted as a frivolous diversion of resources that could otherwise ameliorate terrestrial crises. However, such a perspective is dangerously myopic. The aerospace industry acts as an unparalleled incubator for technological advancement. Innovations birthed from the necessity of surviving the hostile vacuum of space—ranging from advanced water purification systems to miniaturised medical imaging technology—have consistently found ubiquitous applications on Earth, demonstrably elevating our quality of life. Furthermore, gazing outward fosters a unifying, global perspective, reminding us of our shared fragility and collective destiny on this pale blue dot.

Text 2: A Distraction from Earth's Crises
While the romance of exploring the cosmos is undeniably seductive, dedicating astronomical sums to sending probes to barren rocks while our own planet burns is a profound moral failure. We are currently presiding over the sixth mass extinction, accelerating climate breakdown, and yawning chasms of global inequality. Proponents of space travel argue that the spin-off technologies justify the cost, but this is a spectacularly inefficient method of problem-solving. If we wish to cure disease or solve the energy crisis, we should channel those billions directly into terrestrial research rather than hoping for accidental byproducts from vanity projects aimed at colonizing Mars.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Letter: An influential national newspaper recently published an editorial claiming that 'The rapid automation of the workforce through AI will inevitably lead to unprecedented economic prosperity and greater leisure time for all.' You decide to write a letter to the editor. In your letter, you should evaluate this claim, highlighting the potential socioeconomic dangers of rapid automation and proposing systemic changes needed to protect vulnerable workers.

(b) Review: A cultural magazine is running a special feature on 'Challenging Documentaries'. They are asking readers to submit reviews of documentaries that drastically altered their perception of a specific historical or social issue. Write a review describing the documentary, analyzing the techniques the filmmaker used to persuade the audience, and evaluating why the film was so effective at shifting your worldview.

(c) Article: An educational journal is compiling a special edition on 'The Commercialisation of Higher Education'. They are seeking articles examining the trend of universities operating increasingly like profit-driven corporations. Write an article outlining the primary causes of this shift, evaluating its impact on both academic integrity and the student experience, and proposing how universities might balance financial viability with their core educational mission.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways has your hometown evolved since you were a child?
  • How significant is the role of continuous learning in your chosen career path?
  • To what extent do you feel that face-to-face communication is losing its value in modern society?
  • If you could witness the resolution of one major global challenge in your lifetime, what would it be?

Part 2 - Collaborative Task (4 minutes)

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

Candidate A (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate B will then answer a question.

How has the digital age fundamentally altered our conception of personal privacy?
  • The willing surrender of data for convenience
  • Surveillance capitalism and targeted advertising
  • The psychological impact of being constantly observed

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe that complete anonymity on the internet is a right that should be fiercely protected?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

To what extent is the relentless pursuit of economic growth incompatible with environmental sustainability?
  • The myth of decoupling emissions from growth
  • The psychological drivers of hyper-consumerism
  • Transitioning towards circular or steady-state economies

Question for Candidate A: Can technological innovation alone solve the climate crisis without requiring profound changes to human behavior?

General Discussion:

  • Some cultural theorists argue that we are losing the ability to engage in deep, sustained reading. What is your assessment of this?
  • How might the increasing prevalence of algorithmic decision-making impact our sense of moral responsibility?
  • Is the concept of the traditional 'nation-state' becoming obsolete in a hyper-globalised world?
