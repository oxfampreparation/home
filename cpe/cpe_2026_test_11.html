Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: The Case for Mandatory National Service
In an era characterized by deepening social fragmentation and profound political polarization, the reinstatement of mandatory national service offers a crucial mechanism for civic reunification. By requiring young adults to dedicate a year to either military or community service, the state can actively break down entrenched socio-economic bubbles. This shared crucible of responsibility exposes individuals to diverse demographics they would otherwise never encounter, forging a robust, collective national identity. Furthermore, it instils a profound sense of civic duty and discipline in a generation often accused of unprecedented entitlement, ensuring they actively contribute to the societal infrastructure from which they benefit.

Text 2: The Infringement on Liberty
The proposition of mandatory national service represents a gross violation of fundamental individual liberty, substituting personal autonomy with state coercion. Forcing young adults to surrender a vital year of their prime developmental phase disrupts their educational trajectories and delays their entry into a highly competitive global workforce. Moreover, the assertion that forced labour—whether in a military uniform or a community centre—magically engenders genuine patriotism is psychologically naive. True civic engagement cannot be mandated; it must be cultivated through voluntary participation and a genuine belief in the institutions being served. Conscription is a relic of authoritarian regimes, entirely incompatible with the ethos of a modern, free democracy.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Report: Your multinational company is considering a permanent transition to a four-day working week. You have been asked by the board of directors to write a report assessing the feasibility of this change. You should evaluate the potential impact on overall corporate productivity, analyze the likely effects on employee mental health and retention, and provide a clear recommendation on how to implement a pilot program.

(b) Review: A leading science fiction magazine has requested reviews of recent novels that tackle the theme of catastrophic climate change. Write a review of a book you have read, evaluating how effectively the author balances compelling narrative tension with scientific accuracy, and discussing whether the novel ultimately inspires hope or succumbs to ecological despair.

(c) Letter: A national broadsheet has published a controversial article claiming that 'Universities should operate strictly as commercial enterprises, prioritizing highly profitable STEM degrees and entirely defunding the humanities.' Write a letter to the editor robustly challenging this purely utilitarian view of higher education, defending the intrinsic value of critical thinking fostered by arts degrees, and outlining the long-term societal dangers of commercializing academia.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways do you feel your generation’s approach to work differs from that of your parents?
  • How important is it for individuals to have a creative outlet outside of their primary profession?
  • To what extent do you believe that globalization has homogenized local cultures?
  • If you could mandate one book to be read by every politician in the world, what would it be and why?

Part 2 - Collaborative Task (4 minutes)

Here are two photographs illustrating human adaptation to extreme environments. First, talk together about what specific physical and psychological challenges these environments present. Then, decide which environment demands a higher degree of communal cooperation for long-term survival.

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

Candidate A (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate B will then answer a question.

To what extent will the mass production of synthetic meat revolutionize society?
  • The mitigation of catastrophic agricultural emissions
  • The economic devastation of traditional farming communities
  • The psychological barrier of consuming laboratory-grown protein

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe the state should heavily subsidize synthetic meat to drive down consumer prices?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

How should humanity prepare for the transition to a post-work society?
  • The necessity of implementing a Universal Basic Income
  • The psychological crisis of finding meaning without employment
  • The restructuring of the education system away from vocational training

Question for Candidate A: Is a society without mandatory labor inherently destined for intellectual stagnation?

General Discussion:

  • Some economists argue that extreme wealth inequality is actually a vital driver of technological innovation. What is your assessment of this?
  • How might the mainstream acceptance of virtual reality holidays impact the global tourism industry and the environment?
  • Does the preservation of historical architecture unjustifiably hinder the modernization and efficiency of rapidly growing cities?
