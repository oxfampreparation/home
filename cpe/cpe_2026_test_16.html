Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: The Privatization of Water
As global freshwater reserves face unprecedented depletion due to climate change and agricultural over-extraction, the argument for the privatization of water utilities is gaining significant traction. Proponents assert that transferring the management of water infrastructure to private corporations introduces vital market efficiencies. State-run utilities are frequently plagued by chronic underfunding, bureaucratic inertia, and decaying infrastructure. Private enterprises, driven by the necessity of generating a return on investment, are incentivized to aggressively upgrade dilapidated pipe networks, heavily penalize profligate waste, and optimize the purification process. In this view, treating water as an accurately priced economic commodity is the only realistic mechanism to secure its sustainable distribution for future generations.

Text 2: The Right to Water
The commodification of essential biological necessities represents a profound moral failure. Water is not a luxury good; it is an absolute, non-negotiable prerequisite for human survival, and access to it must be enshrined as a fundamental human right. When multinational corporations assume control of municipal water supplies, their overarching legal obligation is to maximize shareholder profit, not to ensure public welfare. This invariably results in extortionate price hikes that disproportionately devastate marginalized communities, forcing low-income families to choose between hydration and basic sanitation. Subjecting the very essence of life to the ruthless mechanics of the free market is fundamentally incompatible with the principles of a just and equitable society.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Report: You have been commissioned by the board of directors of your multinational manufacturing company to write a report on drastically reducing the corporation's carbon footprint. You should evaluate the feasibility of transitioning entirely to renewable energy grids, analyze the financial implications of overhauling the international supply chain, and recommend a structured, five-year implementation strategy.

(b) Review: A prominent arts magazine is publishing a special issue on 'The Death of the Script'. They are seeking reviews of avant-garde theatrical performances that rely entirely on audience interaction and spontaneous improvisation. Write a review of an improvised production you have attended, evaluating how effectively the actors maintained narrative cohesion, and analyzing whether the unpredictability enhanced or detracted from the emotional resonance of the performance.

(c) Letter: Your local municipal council has voted to demolish a culturally significant, though highly controversial, Brutalist concrete library in the city centre to make way for a luxury glass retail complex. Write a letter to the editor of your national newspaper fiercely opposing this decision. You should defend the architectural importance of Brutalism, highlight the civic value of public libraries over commercial retail, and propose a viable strategy for retrofitting the existing structure.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways do you believe our perception of 'success' changes as we grow older?
  • How important is it for societies to maintain traditional ceremonies and rituals?
  • To what extent do you feel that extreme wealth invariably isolates an individual from reality?
  • If you could permanently eliminate one aspect of modern social media, what would it be?

Part 2 - Collaborative Task (4 minutes)

Here are two photographs illustrating different methods of preserving human history. First, talk together about the specific technical and ethical challenges involved in these contrasting preservation methods. Then, decide which method offers a more enduring connection to the past for future generations.

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

Candidate A (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate B will then answer a question.

To what extent has the concept of childhood been irreparably commercialized?
  • The aggressive targeting of toddlers by algorithmic advertising
  • The commodification of play and educational milestones
  • The psychological impact of monetizing family life on social media

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe the state should legally ban all advertising directed at children under twelve?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

How is the rapid advancement of synthetic biology likely to reshape the 21st century?
  • The potential to engineer bespoke microorganisms to consume plastic waste
  • The terrifying existential risk of engineered, targeted pathogens
  • The philosophical dilemma of creating life entirely from scratch

Question for Candidate A: Is the current international regulatory framework adequate to govern synthetic biological research?

General Discussion:

  • Some cultural critics argue that the modern obsession with 'wellness' and self-care is merely a symptom of a deeply sick society. What is your assessment of this?
  • How might the widespread deployment of autonomous military drones permanently alter the ethics of warfare?
  • Does the intense global focus on STEM subjects dangerously devalue the study of human history and philosophy?
