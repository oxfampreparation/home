Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: The Necessity of Universal Healthcare
Treating medical care as a free-market commodity rather than a fundamental human right represents a profound moral failure of modern capitalism. A purely privatized healthcare system inevitably prioritizes profit margins over patient outcomes, incentivizing the denial of coverage for pre-existing conditions and driving up the cost of life-saving pharmaceuticals to extortionate levels. Conversely, robust, state-funded universal healthcare systems pool national resources to ensure that every citizen, regardless of socio-economic status, has guaranteed access to comprehensive medical treatment. This model not only eradicates the terrifying specter of medical bankruptcy but also fosters a healthier, more productive national workforce, proving that socialized medicine is an economic imperative as much as an ethical one.

Text 2: The Efficiency of the Free Market
While the concept of universal healthcare is undeniably utopian, state-monopolized medical systems are chronically inefficient, characterized by cripplingly long waiting lists, suffocating bureaucracy, and severely strained budgets. The absence of market competition actively stifles innovation, leading to a dangerous stagnation in the development of cutting-edge surgical techniques and revolutionary drugs. A heavily regulated but fundamentally privatized system forces medical providers to constantly improve the quality of care to attract patients. It injects vital agility into the healthcare sector, allowing for rapid technological integration and offering patients the ultimate freedom to customize their health plans without burdening the national taxpayer with an unsustainable fiscal deficit.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Report: Your municipal council is considering a radical proposal to permanently ban all private automobiles from the historic city centre to combat severe air pollution. Write a report evaluating the feasibility of this plan. You should analyze the potential economic impact on central retail businesses, propose logistical solutions for residents and delivery services, and make a definitive recommendation.

(b) Review: A leading digital arts magazine is running a special feature on 'Video Games as Philosophy'. They are seeking reviews of science-fiction games that transcend mere entertainment. Write a review of a game you have played, evaluating how successfully its narrative tackles complex themes (such as transhumanism or existentialism), and analyzing how the interactive gameplay mechanics deepen the player's emotional resonance.

(c) Letter: Your national education board has announced plans to severely reduce funding for philosophy and history departments in high schools, redirecting the budget entirely to mandatory coding and robotics classes. Write a letter to the editor of a major newspaper fiercely opposing this utilitarian shift, defending the irreplaceable value of ethical reasoning, and arguing that technological advancement is inherently dangerous without a strong foundation in the humanities.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways do you believe our perception of 'celebrity' has fundamentally changed in the internet era?
  • How important is it for societies to invest heavily in the exploration of deep space?
  • To what extent do you feel that modern consumer culture suppresses genuine individuality?
  • If you could permanently eradicate one specific societal prejudice, what would it be?

Part 2 - Collaborative Task (4 minutes)

Here are two photographs illustrating profoundly different ways humanity processes and stores vast amounts of knowledge. First, talk together about the specific physical and psychological experiences of interacting with information in these two distinct environments. Then, decide which environment offers a more secure method of preserving historical truth for future generations.

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

Candidate A (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate B will then answer a question.

To what extent should art be separated from the moral failings of the artist?
  • The psychological difficulty of enjoying works created by deeply flawed individuals
  • The danger of sanitizing art history through modern ideological censorship
  • The financial implications of boycotting controversial contemporary creators

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe public monuments dedicated to historically problematic figures should be destroyed?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

How is the aggressive globalization of the food industry affecting local culinary traditions?
  • The ecological devastation caused by massive agricultural monocultures
  • The gradual homogenization and loss of unique regional diets
  • The democratization of access to diverse global ingredients

Question for Candidate A: Is the trend towards highly processed, synthetic food an inevitable necessity for feeding a growing population?

General Discussion:

  • Some critics argue that the relentless pursuit of economic growth is fundamentally incompatible with ecological survival. What is your assessment?
  • How might the widespread deployment of autonomous military drones completely alter the psychology of warfare?
  • Does the intense modern focus on 'wellness' actually create a toxic culture of narcissistic self-obsession?
