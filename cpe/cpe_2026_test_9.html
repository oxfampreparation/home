Reading and Use of English Listening Writing Speaking

Reading and Use of English (1 hour 30 minutes)

Listening (about 40 minutes)

The recordings are read aloud by your browser. Each recording can be played twice, as in the real exam. Use headphones.

Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: The Decline of Print Media
The shift towards digital consumption has irrevocably disrupted traditional publishing paradigms. Modern readers exhibit drastically reduced attention spans, fundamentally altering the way information is structured and consumed. Print media, historically the bastion of long-form, rigorously edited investigative journalism, finds itself unable to compete with the sheer velocity of digital news cycles. This relentless pursuit of immediacy over accuracy threatens not only the financial viability of broadsheets but also fundamentally erodes the public’s capacity for sustained, critical analysis of complex global issues.

Text 2: The Rise of Citizen Journalism
Ubiquitous smartphone access has democratized information dissemination, challenging historical journalistic gatekeepers. No longer are audiences reliant on heavily curated narratives dictated by elite editorial boards. Citizen journalists, present at the immediate locus of global events, offer raw, unfiltered perspectives that frequently bypass state censorship. While critics bemoan a lack of professional objectivity, the sheer multiplicity of these decentralized voices provides a significantly more democratic, albeit chaotic, tapestry of global events, fundamentally shifting power from corporate media conglomerates back to the populace.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Report: You have been commissioned by a local government council to write a report on the feasibility of pedestrianizing the central business district. You must evaluate the potential economic impact on local retailers, the logistical challenges of rerouting public transport, and the overarching environmental benefits. Make robust recommendations on whether the project should proceed.

(b) Proposal: An international cultural exchange program is seeking proposals for a festival celebrating the fusion of traditional crafts and modern technology. Write a proposal outlining a specific theme for the festival, detailing three interactive exhibits, and justifying how this event will foster cross-generational engagement in your community.

(c) Letter: A renowned scientific journal recently published an article stating that 'Space exploration is an unethical expenditure of public funds given the pressing humanitarian crises on Earth'. Write a letter to the editor challenging this premise, arguing for the intrinsic value of space exploration as a catalyst for technological innovation and long-term species survival.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways do you think your cultural heritage has shaped your approach to problem-solving?
  • How significant is the impact of algorithmic social media feeds on our political polarization?
  • To what extent do you believe the concept of a 'job for life' is completely obsolete?
  • If you could witness any historical event as an invisible observer, which would you choose and why?

Part 2 - Collaborative Task (4 minutes)

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

Candidate A (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate B will then answer a question.

To what extent should genetic engineering be utilized to enhance human capabilities?
  • The eradication of hereditary diseases
  • The exacerbation of socio-economic inequality
  • The ethical boundaries of 'playing God'

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe cognitive enhancement therapies should be strictly regulated in academic environments?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

How is the rapid urbanization of the developing world reshaping global culture?
  • The erosion of indigenous traditions and dialects
  • The creation of hyper-dense, unsustainable mega-cities
  • The centralization of economic and political power

Question for Candidate A: Is the romanticization of rural life a purely Western luxury?

General Discussion:

  • Some sociologists argue that boredom is an essential catalyst for creativity. What is your assessment of this?
  • How might the deciphering of lost ancient languages fundamentally alter our understanding of human civilization?
  • Does marine archaeology justify its massive expense, given the pressing issues affecting the surface world?
