Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: The Ivory Tower
For centuries, the traditional university has functioned as a sanctuary of pure intellectual pursuit, deliberately insulated from the vulgar demands of the commercial marketplace. The primary role of higher education is not to churn out compliant corporate workers, but to cultivate critical thinkers who can engage with complex philosophical and scientific paradigms. By prioritising the humanities and theoretical sciences, universities protect the intrinsic value of knowledge. Diluting this academic rigour by focusing overwhelmingly on vocational training and immediately 'employable' skills profoundly undermines the intellectual foundations of a sophisticated society.

Text 2: The Pragmatic Shift
In an era defined by rapid technological disruption and escalating economic precariousness, the insistence that universities remain detached from the job market is dangerously obsolete. Students are saddled with crippling debt to finance degrees that frequently hold little utility in the modern workplace. It is a fundamental dereliction of duty for institutions not to equip their graduates with tangible, marketable skills. Higher education must evolve from a theoretical indulgence into a pragmatic incubator for the specific competencies required by contemporary industries, ensuring that academic output directly correlates with economic viability.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Letter: A national broadsheet recently published a provocative article arguing that 'Extreme sports like free-solo rock climbing and base jumping are inherently selfish acts of reckless vanity that should be outlawed to prevent the unnecessary burden on emergency services.' You decide to write a letter to the editor. In your letter, you should fiercely challenge this view, evaluating the psychological drivers behind extreme risk-taking, and arguing for the philosophical importance of individual autonomy and the pursuit of human limits.

(b) Review: A prominent arts and culture website is running a special feature on 'Disappointing Adaptations'. They are seeking reviews of classic novels that have been recently adapted into high-budget films or television series that ultimately failed to capture the essence of the original text. Write a review describing the adaptation, analyzing the specific directorial or casting choices that undermined the narrative, and evaluating why the original source material is superior.

(c) Article: A linguistic journal is compiling an edition on 'The Erosion of Linguistic Diversity'. They are seeking articles examining the rapid disappearance of indigenous and minority languages in the face of globalized English and Spanish. Write an article outlining the primary socioeconomic drivers of this language death, evaluating the profound cultural knowledge lost when a language vanishes, and proposing innovative, community-led strategies for linguistic preservation.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways do you think your generational perspective differs most from that of your parents?
  • How significant is the role of aesthetic beauty in your daily environment?
  • To what extent do you feel that traditional forms of journalism are becoming obsolete?
  • If you could permanently eradicate one specific societal prejudice, what would it be?

Part 2 - Collaborative Task (4 minutes)

Here are some photographs illustrating people coping with extreme weather conditions. First, talk together about how the external environments might dictate the nature of the psychological challenges they face. Then, decide which situation demands the greatest degree of community cooperation to survive.

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

Candidate A (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate B will then answer a question.

To what extent has globalization eroded a distinct sense of national identity?
  • The ubiquity of multinational brands and media
  • The preservation of indigenous languages and customs
  • The rise of reactionary nationalism

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe that a unified global culture is an inevitable and positive outcome of technological progress?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

How has the concept of 'truth' been destabilized in the modern era?
  • The echo-chamber effect of algorithmic social media
  • The proliferation of deep-fakes and AI-generated content
  • The decline of trust in traditional institutional authority

Question for Candidate A: Can critical thinking be effectively taught in schools to combat the spread of misinformation?

General Discussion:

  • Some philosophers argue that true altruism does not exist, and all human actions are fundamentally self-serving. What is your assessment of this?
  • How might the increasing reliance on artificial intelligence in creative fields, such as art and literature, redefine what it means to be an artist?
  • Is the concept of endless economic growth fundamentally a flawed paradigm?
