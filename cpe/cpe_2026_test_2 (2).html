Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: The Eradication of Privacy
In our relentless pursuit of connectivity and convenience, we have unwittingly surrendered our most fundamental right: privacy. The proliferation of digital surveillance, data mining algorithms, and location-tracking applications means that our every move, purchase, and preference is commodified by tech conglomerates. We inhabit a 'glass house' society where anonymity is a relic of the past. Defenders argue this yields highly personalized experiences, yet they ignore the chilling effect such omnipresent observation exerts on free speech and individual autonomy, transforming citizens into mere data points.

Text 2: The Age of Transparency
Lamenting the loss of traditional privacy fundamentally misreads the benefits of our hyper-connected era. The shedding of digital anonymity is ushering in an unprecedented age of transparency and accountability. Open ledgers and social media footprints make it increasingly difficult for corruption, discrimination, and criminal behaviour to remain hidden in the shadows. We are building a society based on radical honesty, where shared data drives medical breakthroughs, optimizes city infrastructure, and fosters global communities. The trade-off for a degree of personal obscurity is a safer, more efficient, and deeply collaborative world.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Letter: An international news magazine recently published an editorial arguing that 'Space exploration is an unjustifiable waste of financial resources in a world plagued by poverty and climate change'. You decide to write a letter to the editor. In your letter, you should challenge their viewpoint, providing two specific examples of how space technology benefits life on Earth, and arguing for the philosophical importance of human exploration.

(b) Review: An arts website is running a feature on 'Transformative Cultural Events'. They want reviews of a live event (e.g., a theatre production, a classical concert, or an art exhibition) that deeply affected you. Write a review describing the event, evaluating the artistic choices made by the creators, and explaining why it had such a profound emotional or intellectual impact on you.

(c) Article: A sociological journal is compiling a special edition entitled 'The Death of the High Street'. They are seeking articles examining the decline of physical retail stores. Write an article outlining the primary causes of this decline, evaluating the social consequences for local communities, and proposing innovative ways that empty retail spaces could be repurposed for public benefit.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways do you think your current lifestyle differs from that of your grandparents when they were your age?
  • How important is it for individuals to continually acquire new skills throughout their lives?
  • To what extent do you feel that modern society values speed over quality?
  • If you could witness any historical event firsthand, which would you choose and why?

General Discussion:

  • Some argue that the constant pursuit of economic growth is fundamentally incompatible with environmental preservation. What is your view?
  • How might the widespread adoption of artificial intelligence fundamentally alter our understanding of human creativity?
  • Is it inevitable that highly developed societies become more secular and less reliant on traditional community structures?
