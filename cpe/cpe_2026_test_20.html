Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: The Democratic Imperative of Mandatory Voting
In democratic societies plagued by chronically low voter turnout, the implementation of mandatory voting is a necessary structural correction. When civic participation becomes entirely optional, the electoral process is inevitably hijacked by highly motivated, often radicalized fringe demographics. This systemic apathy allows wealthy interest groups to disproportionately dictate national policy. Compulsory voting forces the entire socio-economic spectrum into the political arena, compelling governments to genuinely address the nuanced concerns of the marginalized working classes rather than catering exclusively to elite donors. It transforms the act of voting from a mere personal privilege into a fundamental, shared civic duty, essentially anchoring the legitimacy of the state.

Text 2: The Right to Political Abstention
Coercing citizens to the ballot box under the threat of punitive fines is a profound violation of democratic liberty. The right to vote must inherently include the right to consciously abstain. Abstention is frequently a deliberate, powerful political statement—a profound rejection of a corrupted electoral system or a deeply flawed roster of candidates. Mandating participation merely forces millions of disengaged, utterly uninformed individuals to cast random, arbitrary votes, thereby violently diluting the deliberate choices of the politically literate electorate. True democratic engagement cannot be aggressively legislated; it must be organically cultivated through transparent governance, rigorous education, and the presentation of genuinely inspiring political alternatives.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Proposal: Your city council has secured funding to drastically overhaul its outdated, inefficient municipal recycling infrastructure. Write a proposal outlining a radical new strategy. You should evaluate the implementation of automated, AI-driven sorting facilities, propose a community incentivization program to increase household compliance, and justify why this investment will ultimately be financially self-sustaining.

(b) Review: A prominent historical magazine is running a special feature titled 'Shadows and Secrets'. They are seeking reviews of critically acclaimed documentaries that explore the history of international espionage. Write a review describing a documentary you have watched, evaluating how effectively the director utilizes declassified archival footage to build tension, and analyzing the moral ambiguities highlighted by the narrative.

(c) Letter: Your national government has recently proposed implementing a punitive, incredibly high 'sugar tax' on all heavily processed foods to combat the rising healthcare costs of obesity. Write a letter to the editor of a major newspaper fiercely complaining about this policy. You should argue that it is a regressive tax that disproportionately punishes low-income families, outline the failure of the state to subsidize healthy alternatives, and propose a more educational approach to public health.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways do you believe modern society has become overly reliant on instantaneous communication?
  • How important is it for governments to aggressively fund the exploration of the deep ocean?
  • To what extent do you feel that the traditional concept of 'retirement' is becoming obsolete?
  • If you could mandate a single global reform to improve the education system, what would it be?

Part 2 - Collaborative Task (4 minutes)

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

Candidate A (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate B will then answer a question.

To what extent does the concept of intellectual property stifle genuine innovation?
  • The monopolization of life-saving pharmaceutical patents
  • The necessity of financial incentives for corporate research
  • The democratization of information through open-source networks

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe the internet should be treated as a borderless, entirely unregulated zone for sharing ideas?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

How does the modern glorification of extreme sports impact human psychology?
  • The relentless societal pressure to constantly push physical boundaries
  • The commercialization of danger by global energy drink brands
  • The profound psychological clarity achieved through facing absolute risk

Question for Candidate A: Is the desire to participate in life-threatening activities merely a symptom of profound urban boredom?

General Discussion:

  • Some cultural theorists argue that we are entering an era of 'post-truth' politics where objective facts no longer matter. What is your assessment?
  • How might the widespread adoption of lab-grown synthetic organs permanently alter the ethics of the medical transplant industry?
  • Does the intense global focus on highly profitable STEM degrees dangerously devalue the study of human history and philosophy?
