Writing (1 hour 30 minutes)

Part 1 - Discursive Essay (compulsory)

Read the two texts below. Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas in your answers.

Text 1: The Automation of Creativity
The rapid integration of Artificial Intelligence into the realm of artistic creation is frequently hailed as a democratization of art. Proponents argue that complex algorithms now allow individuals entirely lacking in traditional technical skills to manifest profound visual and musical concepts simply by typing a prompt. This technological paradigm shift removes the elitist barriers of formal training, ensuring that raw imagination is the only currency of value. Furthermore, AI acts as a tireless collaborator, generating unprecedented stylistic fusions that push the boundaries of human aesthetic experience far beyond what traditional mediums could ever achieve alone.

Text 2: The Illusion of Authorship
The creeping normalization of AI-generated art represents a fundamental existential threat to human creative integrity. By relying on algorithms that scrape and regurgitate millions of copyrighted works without consent, these systems engage in sophisticated, mechanized plagiarism. More alarmingly, the process entirely severs the crucial emotional link between the creator and the artwork. True art is an arduous, visceral process born of human struggle, joy, and lived experience. Allowing a machine to synthesize an image in seconds completely devalues the profound dedication required to master a craft, replacing genuine artistic intent with hollow, algorithmic mimicry.

Write your essay in 240 - 280 words.

Part 2 - Choose ONE task

Write an answer to one of the questions (a - c) in this part. Write your answer in 280 - 320 words in an appropriate style.

(a) Letter: A national broadsheet recently published an editorial claiming that 'Funneling billions of taxpayer dollars into space exploration is an unconscionable vanity project while terrestrial ecosystems are on the brink of collapse.' You decide to write a letter to the editor fiercely challenging this view, evaluating the indirect technological benefits of aerospace research and arguing for the philosophical necessity of cosmic exploration.

(b) Review: A prestigious arts magazine is running a special feature on 'Theatre that Challenges'. They are seeking reviews of contemporary stage productions that deliberately force the audience out of their comfort zone. Write a review describing the play you attended, analyzing the specific directorial techniques used to disorient or provoke the audience, and evaluating whether these methods were artistically justified or merely gratuitous.

(c) Article: An educational journal is compiling a special edition on 'The Obsolescence of the Physical Book'. They are seeking articles examining the rapid decline of cursive handwriting and physical libraries in the digital age. Write an article outlining the primary cognitive drivers of this shift, evaluating the subtle psychological losses associated with purely digital reading, and proposing strategies to maintain a tactile relationship with literature in a screen-dominated world.

Speaking (16 minutes)

Practise with a partner or teacher. The real test involves two candidates and two examiners.

Part 1 - Interview (2 minutes)

  • In what ways do you feel your generation's attitude towards privacy differs from that of your parents?
  • How significant is the role of aesthetic beauty in your daily environment?
  • To what extent do you believe that traditional forms of print journalism are becoming obsolete?
  • If you could witness the resolution of one major scientific paradox, what would it be?

Part 3 - Long Turn & Discussion (10 minutes)

Candidate A (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate B will then answer a question.

To what extent does extreme wealth insulate individuals from the reality of society?
  • The erosion of empathy and shared experience
  • The disproportionate influence on political infrastructure
  • The philanthropic responsibility of billionaires

Question for Candidate B: Do you believe that the existence of billionaires is a sign of a failing economic system?

Candidate B (2 minutes): Look at the prompt card and talk about it. Candidate A will then answer a question.

How might human cognitive enhancement technologies redefine what it means to be human?
  • The potential eradication of neurological diseases
  • The widening of socioeconomic inequality
  • The loss of authentic, unassisted human achievement

Question for Candidate A: Can we draw a definitive ethical line between medical treatment and human enhancement?

General Discussion:

  • Some philosophers argue that true altruism does not exist, and all human actions are fundamentally self-serving. What is your assessment of this?
  • How might the increasing reliance on algorithmic curation in media limit our exposure to challenging or divergent philosophical viewpoints?
  • Is the concept of 'endless economic progress' a fundamentally destructive myth?
